Master thesis

Constructions of Hamiltonian graphs with prescribed parameters

Extremal graph theory, cages and record graphs, algorithms and experiments.

Aim and motivation

Constructions of cages and record graphs, and the study of their properties, are a very active area in extremal graph theory. For each pair of parameters (k, g) there exist infinitely many (k, g)-graphs. The graph of smallest order n(k, g) among them is a (k, g)-cage. Cages are known only for few parameter pairs; proving that a given graph is a cage is computationally very hard. The smallest known (k, g)-graph not yet proven to be a cage is a record graph rec(k, g). Sachs conjectured that for every k, g there exists a Hamiltonian cage; understanding Hamiltonicity of cages and record graphs is therefore a natural and difficult problem.

Aim. Develop graph constructions that, given a (k, g)-graph, produce a Hamiltonian (k, g)-graph, with emphasis on smallest possible order or non-trivial upper bounds. This requires designing and implementing construction algorithms and running substantial computational experiments to test graph properties.

Motivation. The project sits between combinatorial construction theory and computational mathematics: conjectures and bounds drive the search for explicit graphs, and good algorithms turn those searches into evidence and, ideally, into proofs or sharper bounds.
